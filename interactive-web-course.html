Lesson 1 of 6

🪖 Gear Up! E-Bike Safety Equipment

An e-bike is faster and heavier than a regular bike — so your safety gear has to work harder too. In this lesson you’ll meet every piece of safety equipment: the gear you wear and the gear that lives on your bike — and learn how to check it all before every ride.

🪖 Gear #1: Your Helmet — Pick an E-Bike-Rated One!

Your e-bike goes faster than a regular pedal bike — so your helmet has to be tested for those faster speeds. Not every helmet is! Here’s how to tell them apart:

🐢

Regular Bike Helmet

Made and safety-tested for slower pedal-bike speeds. Good protection on a regular bike — but an e-bike can zoom faster than this helmet was tested for.

⚡

E-Bike-Rated Helmet — “NTA-8776”

Safety-tested for faster e-bike speeds — up to 28 mph — with extra coverage at the temples and the back of your head. This is the safer pick for e-bike riding!

🔍
Helmet Ratings — check the sticker inside!
🏷️ Be a sticker detective: with a grown-up, look inside the helmet for its safety certification sticker. If it says “NTA-8776”, it’s e-bike-rated — tested for speeds up to 28 mph, with extra coverage guarding your temples and the back of your head. No sticker? That helmet hasn’t passed the safety tests — don’t ride in it.
🎯 Fit matters most of all — even the best-rated helmet only protects you if it fits right, so do the 3 fit steps below every time.
💥 Crashed it? Replace it! If a helmet ever takes a hit in a crash, it’s done — replace it, even if it looks fine. Its protection can be used up after one big bonk.

✅ Got the Right Helmet? Now Fit It Right — 3 Steps

🎩

1. Sit It Level

Put your helmet on level — flat like a hat, never tipped back like a baseball cap. The front edge sits about 2 fingers above your eyebrows, so your forehead is protected too.

✌️

2. Straps Make a “V”

The side straps form a “V” shape, and the bottom of the V sits right under your ears. That V is what holds the helmet in place if you fall.

🔒

3. Buckle & Shake Test

Click the buckle under your chin and tighten until only 2 fingers fit under the strap — no more. Then shake your head: if the helmet stays put, the fit is perfect. Wiggles? Tighten it, or ask a grown-up about a different size.

💡 Gear That Lives on Your Bike

💡

Lights: White Front, Red Back

Your e-bike needs a white light in front and a red light in back. Switch them on whenever it’s dim — dusk, dawn, rain, or fog. In many states, lights after dark are the law, not a choice!

🔆

Reflectors — Your Backup Glow

Your bike came with reflectors — in front, in back, on the wheels, and on the pedals. They bounce car headlights back at drivers so you glow in the dark. Keep them on your bike and wipe them clean — a muddy reflector can’t shine.

🔔

Bell or Horn — Be Heard

Ring your bell or call “On your left!” so walkers and drivers hear you coming. A friendly “Excuse me!” works too. Being heard is safety gear, just like being seen.

✋

Brakes That Stop Smoothly

Squeeze both brakes — your bike should stop smoothly, without squealing or pulling to one side. Brakes are gear too: if they feel weak or spongy, there’s no ride until a grown-up fixes them.

🔋

Battery & Charging Safety

Charge only with the charger that came with your bike, with a grown-up nearby — never overnight while everyone sleeps, and never on a bed or couch. If a battery looks swollen or cracked, or smells strange, don’t touch it — tell a grown-up right away. And squeeze the tires: firm = ready, squishy = no ride.

Lesson 3 of 6

🚦 Rules of the Road

On the road, you’re a driver — so you follow driver rules. Be predictable: that means no surprises for anyone around you.

➡️

Ride WITH Traffic

Always ride on the right side, in the same direction as cars. Riding toward traffic is dangerous — drivers aren’t looking for you there, and you have no time to react.

🛑

Stop Means STOP

Stop signs and red lights are for bikes too. Stop fully, look left → right → left again, look over your shoulder, then go when it’s clear.

👀

Intersections = Danger Zone

Most crashes happen where roads cross. Slow down, make eye contact with drivers, and never assume a driver sees you — even if they’re looking your way.

📵

Eyes Up, Phone Away

Two hands on the handlebars, eyes scanning ahead. No phones, no headphones blasting — you need to hear traffic, bells, and people.

⚡ Know Your E-Bike Class — every e-bike is one of these!

1️⃣

Class 1 — Pedal-Assist

The motor helps only while you pedal, up to 20 mph. No pedaling, no power! Great for bike paths in most places.

2️⃣

Class 2 — Throttle

Has a throttle — twist or push it and the bike goes without pedaling, up to 20 mph. It can also pedal-assist. Squeeze gently — throttle power can surprise you!

3️⃣

Class 3 — Speed Pedal-Assist

Pedal-assist up to 28 mph — the fastest class! In most states it’s for older riders (often 16+), with extra helmet rules, and it’s usually not allowed on sidewalks or shared paths.

🏷️
Where’s your class? Most e-bikes have a sticker on the frame that says Class 1, 2, or 3. Find yours with a grown-up — your class decides where you may ride and which rules apply to you. No sticker? Check the bike’s manual or the maker’s website.
🔍
Spot Check: Is It Really an E-Bike? Some super-powerful bikes are sold online wearing an “e-bike” costume — but they’re really something else! Be a detective and run these three checks with a grown-up:
  • ✅
    Working pedals — real pedals you can pedal, just like a regular bike.
  • ⚡
    A motor of 750 watts or less — check the label or the seller’s page to find the motor size.
  • 🏷️
    A class label — a sticker that says Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3, with its top speed (20 mph for Class 1 & 2, 28 mph for Class 3).
The big rule: No working pedals, or a motor over 750 watts? That’s an e-moto — a real motorcycle in the eyes of the law. It needs a motorcycle license (age 16+), registration, and insurance — and it is not for kids.
Not sure? When in doubt, ask a grown-up to check with the seller before anyone rides.

⚡ Two Ways an E-Bike Gives You Power

🦵

Pedal-Assist — You Pedal, It Helps

Class 1 and Class 3 e-bikes only add power while your legs are pedaling — like a super-strong friend pushing gently. Stop pedaling and the help stops. Class 1 helps up to 20 mph; Class 3 up to 28 mph.

✊

Throttle — Twist & Go (Gently!)

Class 2 e-bikes have a throttle on the handlebar. Twist or push it and the bike moves even if you don’t pedal, up to 20 mph. Throttle power arrives fast — always start with a gentle squeeze, feet ready, looking where you want to go.

🎂
Age rules are real rules: In many states, Class 3 e-bikes (28 mph) are only for riders 16 and older, and some states set minimum ages for other classes too. Helmets are required for kids in many states — and for everyone on Class 3 in several. Grown-ups: find your state’s exact rules in Parents: Local Laws.

Lesson 4 of 6

🚶 Sidewalks & Crosswalks

Sidewalks belong to people walking. If your town allows bikes there, you’re a guest — ride like one: slow, polite, and ready to walk your bike.

🐢

Walking Speed Near People

Slow to a walking pace near pedestrians, driveways, and shop doors. Pass wide, ring your bell early, and say “Excuse me, on your left!” with a smile.

🚶‍♀️

Walk It at Crosswalks

The safest move: get off and walk your bike across in the crosswalk. You become a pedestrian — drivers expect walking speed, and you can stop instantly.

🏠

Watch Every Driveway

Cars back out of driveways without seeing small riders. Treat every driveway like a mini-street: slow down, look both ways, listen for engines.

🐕

Give Everyone Space

Dogs, toddlers, strollers, and people using wheelchairs or canes need extra room and no surprises. If it’s crowded — walk your bike. That’s what smart riders do.

🏘️
Important: In many towns, e-bikes are not allowed on sidewalks at all because they’re faster and heavier than regular bikes — and Class 3 e-bikes are almost never allowed on sidewalks or shared paths. Check your local rule — when in doubt, use the bike lane or walk your bike on the sidewalk.

Lesson 5 of 6

🅿️ Parking Lot Smarts

Parking lots look calm — but they’re one of the trickiest places to ride. Cars back up, doors swing open, and drivers are looking for parking spots, not bikes.

🚗
The golden rule of parking lots: Assume no driver can see you. You are small, cars are tall, and backup lights mean “MOVE AWAY — don’t try to sneak past.”
🔴 Backing-Up Cars

White lights on the back of a car = it’s about to reverse. Stop and wait far behind it — never ride behind a car with its engine on and a driver inside.

🚪 Swinging Doors & Trunks

People pop out from between parked cars without looking. Ride down the middle of the driving lane, slowly — not weaving between parked cars.

👀 Blocked Views

Tall SUVs and trucks hide you completely. At every corner of the lot, slow down and peek before you roll — like a mini-intersection.

🛒 Carts, Kids & Shoppers

Loose carts roll, kids run to cars, and shoppers carry bags that block their view. Go slow — walking speed — in busy lots.

➡️ Follow the Arrows

Lots have one-way lanes and arrows too. Follow them, use the marked driving lanes, and cross at the lot’s crosswalks when you can.

🚲 Park It Right

Park at a bike rack if there is one. No rack? Ask where bikes go. Never block doors, ramps, accessible spots, or lie your bike where someone can trip over it.

🔍 Tool 1: Recall Checker — Is Our E-Bike Recalled?

Sometimes a bike (or its battery) turns out to have a dangerous problem, and the safety experts at the U.S. CPSC say “stop riding and fix it.” That’s called a recall or a safety warning. Find your bike’s info, type it in, and tap the big button — you’ll get a clear STOP, CHECK, or NO MATCH answer.

👀 First, find your bike’s info — here’s where it hides:

🏷️

Brand + Model

Printed right on the frame — look on the long tube and near the seat. It might say something like “RadRunner 2” or “Turbo Como SL.”

🔋

Battery Model

On a sticker on the back of the battery. It’s a code, like “RP-1304.” Take the battery off with a grown-up to peek.

🔢

Serial Number

A long number stamped under the bike, where the pedals spin, or near the back wheel. Shops and makers use it to ID your exact bike.

This checker compares your answers against 6 CPSC e-bike recalls and safety warnings checked on October 22, 2026 (Rad Power Bikes batteries, Ridstar Q20/Q20 Lite, Specialized Turbo Como SL, Urban Arrow cargo bikes, and two Trek/Electra recalls). New recalls happen — a “no match” answer means no match in this list, not a guarantee. Always double-check the full official list at CPSC.gov e-bike recalls, and ask a bike shop if you’re ever unsure.
