01 · Helmet

Buy for fit, not for the brand.

A helmet only protects if it fits this head, carries the right label, and suits how they ride. Price and brand don't tell you any of that.

  1. Measure their headSoft tape around the head, just above the eyebrows. Buy by centimeters, not by age.
  2. Check the label insideU.S. helmets must carry a CPSC sticker. NTA 8776 means it was also tested at e-bike speeds, worth it for Class 2 and 3 bikes.
  3. Try it on togetherLevel, straps in a V under each ear, one or two fingers under the buckle, no sliding when they shake their head. Lesson 1 walks your rider through it.
  4. Check for recallsSearch the exact model on the CPSC recall site before you buy.

03 · Pads

Wrists, knees, elbows.

Most falls are low and slow, and hands go out first. Wrist guards matter most for younger riders. Hard caps slide on pavement instead of grabbing.

07 · Battery

Charging is your job.

Lithium e-bike batteries cause house fires when they're damaged, overcharged or charged with the wrong gear. These habits prevent most of it.

  1. Use the charger that came with the bikeOr a replacement the manufacturer approves. Never a generic one that happens to fit.
  2. Charge while someone's awakeNot overnight, and unplug when it's full.
  3. Hard surface, away from exitsNot on a bed or couch, and not blocking the way out of a room.
  4. Retire a damaged batterySwollen, cracked, leaking or smelling odd: stop using it and take it to household hazardous waste. Never the trash.
  5. Look for UL 2849 or UL 2271The safety certifications for e-bike electrical systems and batteries.
